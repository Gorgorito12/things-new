Reply to JTMaston, round of 2026-11-24: oil display (#103) and global gathering rates (#105), the DLL part
==========================================================================================================

Tested in single player in a copy of WoL. The panel art and layout are a separate part (the oil panel pack).


#103 "oil display": the panel without oil before the Great War, with oil after
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
New key in our DLL, techGadgets. It is generic: it ties any interface gadget to a tech.

    techGadgets <Tech> <GadgetAfter> [<GadgetBefore>]

While the tech is not active for the player, GadgetBefore is shown and GadgetAfter hidden; once it is active, the
reverse. For the oil row it is one line per resource panel variant (12), with the tech GreatWarizeALL, which every
route to the Great War activates (the Great War button, the Tupi button, the AI's alternative and the allies' card;
the engine's age does not change with the Great War, so the tech is the signal):

    techGadgets GreatWarizeALL SettlerCountDialog-defaultPanelEurMax-postGW SettlerCountDialog-defaultPanelEurMax-preGW
    ... (the 12 lines are in the pack's README)

The pack makes each panel variant a holder with two children: "-postGW" (today's panel, with oil) and "-preGW"
(no oil row, the other rows spread). The layout shows postGW by default, so without the key, or with an older DLL,
the panel is exactly today's. You can use the same key for anything else you want to tie to a tech.

Tested in a copy of WoL with a test layout (each of the 12 panel variants split in two, the oil amount left out of
the pre-Great War half): the panel without oil until a trigger researched GreatWarButton, which activates
GreatWarizeALL; the panel with oil from that moment; the same after saving and loading. With the key off, today's
panel all along. Videos: techGadgets-wol and its control in the guide.
Not tested: switching between the full and minimized interface inside one game (the game reads that option at
startup; each variant has its own two children, so each variant switches on its own), and observer mode (multiplayer
only; the observer has its own interface files).


#105 "global gathering": a rate display like DE's, and would it lag?
--------------------------------------------------------------------
What DE shows (read from DE's own interface files): an option "Resource Gather Per Minute" in the interface
options; with it on, a small "(+N)" right of each amount in the resource panel, per
minute. How DE computes the number (gross or net, over how long) we could not find: DE's exe is very large and
partly encrypted, and the code that fills that text did not turn up.

What you get: new key incomeRates and a text for the panel, {uhcIncome(food)} (any resource: food, wood, gold, fame
for oil, skillpoints for faith, xp). As the user chose for WoL: per second with one decimal, under each amount, and a
tooltip line "Income: +N.N per second". It counts what the player's villagers gather, its AutoGather units
(factories, banks, trading posts...) produce, and its trickles, over the last 20 seconds of game time; also what
our DLL's own keys pay (unit resource auras, kill bounties, pillage). It does not count costs, upkeep, tributes, the
market, shipments or treasures: it is income, not the change of the stockpile. With shared resources it is the
team's, as the amount is. Optional: incomeRates perMinute for whole numbers per minute, like DE.

Would it lag? No. Our DLL counts income in the single function every income goes through (one check of who called
it on each call), so it never walks units, whatever the number of players, villagers or trickles. Measured in a new
8-player skirmish (you and 7 European AIs, Acre), two runs with the key on and two with it off, 2 minutes each from
minute 5:
- frames over 50 ms: 8 and 6 with the key, 7 and 9 without (they are the AIs' own spikes, there either way);
- the simulation's time per second: 307 / 259 ms with the key, 286 / 313 ms without (the run-to-run noise is larger
  than any difference);
- measured inside the game: up to 14,200 income payments a minute by minute 8, and 0.3 to 0.8 ms per minute spent
  counting them, about 13 microseconds per second of game time.

Tested: the rate equals what the stockpile gains (wood from 5 villagers: 185.4 counted, +185.4 in the stockpile in
60 s; a Factory's gold: 268.2 and +268.2; the XP trickle: 120.1 and +120.1). Videos: incomeRates-wol and its control.
Note for the layout: with the key off the game prints an unknown text as written, so the pack's "+{uhcIncome(food)}"
needs the key on.
