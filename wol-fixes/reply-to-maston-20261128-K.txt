Culture UI (your answers of 2026-11-26): the DLL part is done and tested. The art is yours to make; until then
nothing changes in the game.

How it works:
- A new DLL key, cultureUI. data\uhc\cultureui.xml lists the nine families of your Great War techs (EU, AN, LA, NS,
  AF, AS, ME, NA, PO) and their civs.
- For a family, the artist ships a copy of an interface texture with the family's suffix next to the original, as a
  loose file. For example, art\ui\ingame\ingame_ui_small_01_ypack_as.ddt is the Asian family's version of
  ingame_ui_small_01_ypack.ddt.
- From the Great War on, the game uses the _gw copy (ingame_ui_small_01_ypack_as_gw.ddt). If there is no _gw copy,
  it keeps the family's copy; if there is no family copy, today's texture.
- Only textures change; the layouts stay as they are. So a copy must keep the original's size and layout. The
  Photoshop templates do that: one PSD per texture, with every piece outlined and named.
- A family with no files keeps today's interface. You can start with one texture and add the rest later.
- The switch at the Great War is instant, with no reload. A saved game loaded after the Great War opens with the
  Great War look.
- No style for observers (the default look, as you asked), for the normal (big) interface, or for civs in no family.
- The look changes during a game only. The main menu, the home city and the post-game screens keep theirs.
- If an artist makes a copy of a texture that menus share with the HUD (the menu buttons, for example), the in-game
  menus show it too. A HUD-only texture changes only the HUD.
- Display only: nothing in the simulation changes, and it costs nothing measurable (the same frame rate with the key
  on and off).

Tested in a copy of WoL, single player. The two test styles in the pictures are deliberately loud recolours, made by
a script only to show the switch; they are not proposals:
- Chinese: the Asian test look from the start, then its Great War look when the Great War is researched;
- saved after the Great War and loaded: the Great War look at once;
- the next game, Ethiopians (no test art for the Africans): today's look;
- Afghans: the Middle Eastern test look, then its Great War look;
- the normal interface: today's look;
- the key off: today's look before and after the Great War;
- next to your own DLL (our dinput8.dll build): works the same;
- the AI keeps playing.

Pictures: cultureUI-wol.gif (Chinese, the switch at the Great War), cultureUI-wol-control.gif (key off),
cultureUI-screens.jpg (every case above), A3_test_styles_preview.png (the two test styles before and after the Great

Observers: the DLL uses the same rule as your DLL: the map's observer mode creates a trigger named StartInsertion,
and the observers are the players from where the team changes (slot 3, 5 or 7). I could not test it in single
player, since an observer needs a second player.
