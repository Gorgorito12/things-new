Reply to JTMaston, round of 2026-11-25: quick-arrival in capitals (#99), space between income and stock (#105),
and what a themed interface per culture would cost (#103)
=============================================================================================================

Tested in single player in a copy of WoL 1.0.15c, 1920x1080.


#99 "Quick arrival card": QUICK-ARRIVAL in capitals
---------------------------------------------------
Done. The line the DLL adds now reads:

    This is a QUICK-ARRIVAL card.

Checked in game on both screens that show it: the in-game shipment panel and "Build a Deck", on an infinite card
(Crates of 300 food) and a card sent once (Chests of 700 coin). Pictures: quickArrivalCards-panel-infinite.jpg,
quickArrivalCards-panel-once.jpg and quickArrivalCards-deck-builder*.jpg in the guide.
If you use "quickArrivalCards stringid N" (the line taken from your string table, to translate it), the DLL shows
your string exactly as written, so the capitals go in your string. Your current wol.cfg has no quickArrivalCards
line, so with the new DLL you get the capitals without changing anything.


#105 "global gathering": more space between the income and the stock value
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Status: the new layout is made and tested; it goes into the oil panel pack (apply_fix.py --rates) once approved.

Where it overlapped: in the panels whose rows are too low for two lines (the 7-row Asian and Native panels with
faith, default and advanced: 8 of the 24 panels with faith; your advanced Native panel: 1 of the 24 without), the
rate sat in a column starting
just right of the amount, so a long amount ran into it. The other panels put the rate under the amount and had no
overlap.

The fix, in those panels only (every other panel is unchanged):
- the rate is set to the panel's right edge (right-aligned), so the amount grows to the right and the rate to the
  left, away from each other;
- the amounts, and the other numbers of that column (villager count, dance value), sit a little closer to their
  icons, which gives about 9 more pixels at 1920x1080.

Tested with every resource (food, wood, gold, oil, export, faith) at 99,999,992 and gold at 100,000,1xx (9 digits,
while factories made +4.6/s), all 24 panels of both modes (with and without faith) on screen at once: nothing
overlaps or is cut, the amounts share one left edge and the rates one right edge.
Note: the game keeps resources as 32-bit floats, so 99,999,999 cannot be shown (the nearest values are 99,999,992
and 100,000,000). The limit of the new layout: 9 digits next to a rate with two digits before the point ("+12.5")
would touch, and 8 digits should fit with any rate up to "+99.9" (both estimated from the digit widths; seen in
game: 8 digits beside +0.0 and 9 digits beside +4.6).


#103 "oil display": how much would a themed interface per culture cost?
-----------------------------------------------------------------------
Short answer: affordable. Measured with a synthetic copy (the whole in-game interface copied 8 times, one per
culture, each copy with its own textures), against today's interface, in alternating games. Full report with the
numbers: wol-fixes\ui-per-culture-cost-20261125.txt.

- What the original game does: only the resource panel changes with the culture. uimainnew.xml has 12 copies of it
  (European / Asian / Native x default / advanced x full / minimized); the game shows one by the options and by
  whether the civ counts as native (your NativeCivs line) or Asian (your asianCivs line), else European. That gives
  three looks for the resource panel, for free, but no fourth culture and no other panel.
- With techGadgets (one hidden holder per culture in uimainnew.xml, one cfg line per culture):
  - memory: the game loads a gadget's textures only when the gadget is first shown. Eight hidden copies (63.5 MB of
    textures on disk) added about 10 MB; showing one culture's copy added 14 MB. A player only sees his own culture,
    so the real cost is one culture's art, about 5-15 MB;
  - speed: no measurable change in fps or in the game's update time; the key itself spends a few microseconds per
    frame for 8 lines;
  - loading: no measurable change;
  - 32-bit room: the game used about 1.25-1.3 GB of its 4 GB in the test, so this is far from the limit.
- One rule: one techGadgets line per culture holder, not one per small gadget. We tried 792 lines (one per piece):
  the key could not find most nested names, kept looking them up, and the game slowed to seconds per frame.
- You need a tech per culture that its civs have from the start (for example one tech per culture that each civ's
  Age0 tech activates).
