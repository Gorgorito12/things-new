1. t now follows the factor
---------------------------
Write S(t) = b^0 + b^1 + ... + b^(t-1). This is how many minks a nursery has after t steps from 0 (S(0) = 0).
For any factor, t is the largest t with S(t) <= factor, and the next step adds S(t+1) - S(t), which is b^t for a whole
b. t is not stored any more: it is worked out from the current factor every time. Anything that changes the factor
moves t with it: a sale, a shipment, a garrisoned goat, a button, a tech, a capture (and a tech that changes b).

With b = 2, S = 0, 1, 3, 7, 15, 31...

    minks      t    next step adds    then
    0          0    1                 1
    1 or 2     1    2                 3 or 4
    3 to 6     2    4                 7 to 10
    7 to 14    3    8                 15 to 22 (cut at the max)

Example: a nursery at 10 sold down to 2 is back at t = 1. It grows 2 -> 4 -> 8 -> 10, no longer 2 -> 10 in one step.
In the test the log showed it after a sale ("10 -> 6/10, t 3 -> 2", then +4) and after a shipment that took 2
("10 -> 8, t 3 -> 3", then +8, cut at 10).

A b that is not a whole number: minks are whole, so S is counted in whole minks. t is the largest t with
floor(S(t)) <= factor, and a step adds floor(S(t+1)) - floor(S(t)). With b = 1.5, S = 0, 1, 2.5, 4.75, 8.125, 13.19...
(whole: 0, 1, 2, 4, 8, 13). From 0 the steps add 1, 1, 2, 4, 5. A nursery at 3 is at t = 2 and adds 2 (to 5); a nursery
at 5 is at t = 3 and adds 4 (to 9).
b = 1: every step adds 1. b below 1: S never passes 1 / (1 - b), so growth stops there (b = 0.5 stops at 1 mink).
The max plays no part in t: a step cut at the max leaves the nursery at the max, and a nursery above a lowered max
keeps its minks and does not grow.


2. The pie, option B
--------------------
The Nursery's pie is now a small square right after the row, "3/10 minks", in both the minimized and the normal
interface. It is the same blue overlay of a training unit: blue at half transparency over grey, in a thin gold frame.
The clear part opens clockwise from 12 o'clock toward the next step. It is placed as an icon in the row's text, so it
sits on the row's line and is as tall as the line, at any resolution: 17 px in the minimized row and 22 px in the
normal panel at 1920 x 1080, 14 and 17 px at 1366 x 768. It shows only while the nursery grows; at the max there is no
pie.

What you need to copy: one folder, art\ui\uhc\pie\ (32 small textures, pie_00.ddt to pie_31.ddt, in the release zip),
into the game's art\ui\uhc\. Every player needs it. Without it, the pie goes back over the portrait, as before.

GarrisonConvertTo keeps its pie over the portrait, as you said.


3. Your question "What units are we talking about?"
---------------------------------------------------
We meant the units a <NurseryFactorCommand> button makes at the nursery, its unit="..." count="...". For example
SellMinks -> 2 CrateofCoin. Crates use no population, but a mod could make a button that spends minks for Settlers or
soldiers. Those used to appear even above the population cap.

We now treat them like GarrisonConvertTo's new rule. A button whose units would not fit under the population cap
does nothing, and it is greyed (its icon darkened) with "Population cap reached" in red in its rollover. It comes back
to normal as soon as there is room. All the units of one click must fit: a button for 3 Settlers needs 3 free
population. Units with no population, like crates, are never stopped.
In the test, a "1 mink for 3 Settlers" button was greyed while the player had no house, came back when a House was
built, and then made 3 Settlers beside the Bank.

If you meant something else, or prefer that such buttons ignore the cap (or make as many as fit), tell us.


4. As you answered (no change)
------------------------------
- A unit sent into a full nursery stays inside as a normal garrison; with room for only part of its amount it is
  taken and the factor stops at the max.
- The second rollover line stays when the nursery is full.
- Any player's matching unit counts, an ally's too. In the test an ally's Settler that entered the Bank was taken
  (1 -> 2/10). Note that a goat changes owner by itself to the player whose units are nearest. So an ally's goat that
  reaches your nursery is usually already yours when it enters.


Also checked
------------
- A saved game kept the minks and the time to the next step.
- 16 growing Banks cost nothing measurable (update 0.2 ms per frame with the feature and without).
- The German AI played normally on a real map (20 Settlers and 3 Manors at 6 min 44 s), with no script error.
- Single player only; multiplayer is not tested.
